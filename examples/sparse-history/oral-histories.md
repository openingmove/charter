# Oral Histories, collected 2019 to 2023

The society records a conversation with an older resident every few months. What follows is the tidied-up substance of the ones we have transcribed so far, in the order we recorded them.

## Maud Ferriby, recorded in spring 2019

Maud was born in 1936 on Quay Street, above what was then the bicycle shop in the old Customs Shed, and worked at the cannery from the age of sixteen until it closed. She remembers the whistle going at seven, noon and five, and says the whole town ran on it: school started when it blew, and the tide tables on the harbour wall were chalked up by the cannery's clerk. She talks warmly about Dorothea Quillfeather, who she knew as "Miss Q" and who once paid for a girl's train fare to the city out of her own pocket so she could take up a scholarship. About the closure in 1958 she is blunter. The notice went up on a Friday, the last tins were packed on a Wednesday, and by Christmas half the girls she had worked with had moved away.

## Tobias Wren, recorded in autumn 2019

Tobias kept the Marsh Gardens for the council for thirty-one years before he retired in 2008. The Marsh Gardens are the low ground where the flood of 1887 did its worst, and Tobias is certain that the soil there is still saltier than anywhere else in town. He says the best tomatoes he ever grew came from a plot beside the allotment shed and the worst leeks came from the plot next to it, forty feet away, and that no one has ever explained it. He also tells the story, which we have not been able to confirm, that a brass bell hangs in the old boathouse beside the gardens and that it came from the church of St Brannoc after the tower was repaired.

## Ines and Gabriel Okonkwo-Hale, recorded in summer 2021

Ines and Gabriel moved to Saltmarsh in 1994 and bought the old customs house on the New Wall, which was then a ruin with a tree growing through the roof. They talk about the restoration with some exhaustion and a lot of pride. The first winter they lived in two rooms with a stove; the second they found a bricked-up cellar with a stack of nineteenth-century oyster crates, which they donated to the society. Gabriel founded the Saltmarsh Regatta in 1997 as a way of meeting the neighbours. The first one had nine boats and a picnic. The most recent, in 2022, had sixty-four boats, a brass band and a queue for the ferry.

## Cornelius Baptiste, recorded in winter 2023

Cornelius is the oldest person we have recorded so far, ninety-four at the time. His grandfather rang the bell at St Brannoc's the night of the flood after Alma Voss grew too tired, and Cornelius insists the family story is that Alma stopped for an hour at four in the morning and a boy took over. We have put the claim in the file without comment. He also remembers the demolition of the cannery in 1961 and watching the chimney come down from the sea wall with his father, who did not speak for the rest of the evening.
